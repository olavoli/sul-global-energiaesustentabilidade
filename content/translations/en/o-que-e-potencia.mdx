Imagine two identical elevators carrying the same load from the ground floor to the tenth floor.

Both start from the same height and arrive at the same destination. Disregarding efficiency differences, the variation in gravitational potential energy of the load is the same in both cases.

But there is a difference: one elevator makes the trip in 10 seconds, while the other takes 30 seconds.

The first one needs to transfer the same amount of energy in less time. Therefore, it develops greater **power**.

<Callout title="The essential difference">

**Energy is related to the quantity transferred or transformed. Power indicates how quickly this transfer or transformation occurs.**

</Callout>

## What is power?

Average power is the ratio between the amount of energy transferred or transformed and the time interval:

**P = ΔE / Δt**

In this relationship, **P** represents power, **ΔE** represents the energy variation and **Δt** represents the time interval.

In the International System, the unit of power is the **watt (W)**:

**1 W = 1 J/s**

This means that a system operating at 1 watt transfers or transforms energy at a rate of 1 joule per second.

## Does a more powerful device always use more energy?

Not necessarily. The operating time also matters.

Consider two devices performing the same task:

- device A: 1 kW for 1 hour;
- device B: 2 kW for 30 minutes.

In the first case, **E = 1 kW × 1 h = 1 kWh**. In the second case, **E = 2 kW × 0.5 h = 1 kWh**.

Both use the same amount of energy, although the second operates with twice the power. It transfers this energy in half the time.

## So what is kWh?

The **kilowatt (kW)** is a unit of power. The **kilowatt-hour (kWh)** is a unit of energy.

**E = P × t**

Thus, maintaining a power of 1 kW for 1 hour corresponds to 1 kWh of energy. Since an hour has 3,600 seconds:

**1 kWh = 3.6 MJ**

That's why the electricity bill records consumption in kWh. It accounts for the energy associated with the power used over time, not just the nominal power of the devices.

## Power is everywhere

Power allows comparing the speed at which very different systems transfer or transform energy.

An LED can operate with a few watts. An appliance can require hundreds or thousands of watts. An industrial motor can work on the scale of kilowatts or megawatts, while large generating units reach even larger scales.

The physical quantity still represents the same idea: **energy per unit of time**.

Therefore, a plant has installed power expressed in MW, while the energy generated during a certain period can be expressed in MWh or GWh. The first measure indicates the generation rate; the second, the quantity generated in the considered interval.

## Power is not efficiency

A more powerful device is not necessarily more efficient.

**Power** describes a rate of energy transfer or transformation. **Efficiency** compares how much of the energy supplied to the system results in the considered useful effect.

Two motors can provide the same mechanical output power and have different efficiencies. The less efficient one will need to receive more energy to offer the same useful result.

This difference is directly related to the concept discussed in [Why is no machine 100% efficient? (in Portuguese)](/artigo/por-que-nenhuma-maquina-e-100-eficiente).

## Energy and power tell different stories

We can summarize it like this:

**Energy: how much.**

**Power: how fast.**

A reservoir can store a large amount of potential energy and release it slowly. Another system can have less available energy, but transfer it quickly and develop great power during a short interval.

Knowing only the available energy does not completely describe the behavior of a system. We also need to know over what time interval that energy can be transferred or transformed.

## In summary

<KeyPoints>

- Power measures the speed of an energy transfer or transformation.
- One watt is equal to one joule per second.
- Kilowatt is a unit of power; kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy.
- The energy used depends on both power and operating time.
- Greater power does not mean, by itself, greater efficiency.

</KeyPoints>
